A social media post receives 500 likes.

Your website gains 2,000 new visitors.

Your follower count increases by 15%.

These numbers may look impressive, but do they mean your business is growing?

Not necessarily.

Digital marketing should support business objectives. Those objectives might include generating qualified leads, increasing sales, improving customer retention, or reducing customer acquisition costs.

Likes and followers can provide useful information about audience engagement, but they should not be the only measures of marketing success.

Here are the metrics businesses should pay closer attention to.

## 1. Qualified Leads

A lead is someone who expresses interest in your product or service.

However, not every enquiry is a suitable customer.

A qualified lead meets important business requirements, such as having the right needs, budget, location, or purchasing intent.

Track:

- Total enquiries.
- Qualified enquiries.
- Lead sources.
- Cost per qualified lead.
- Lead-to-customer conversion rate.

This helps identify which marketing channels attract the right people. And once those leads arrive, make sure your follow-up doesn't let them slip away. See [Why Businesses Lose Leads After Their First Enquiry](insights/why-businesses-lose-leads/).

## 2. Conversion Rate

Your conversion rate measures the percentage of people who complete a desired action.

That action could be:

- Completing a contact form.
- Booking a consultation.
- Requesting a quote.
- Making a purchase.
- Signing up for a newsletter.

A simple conversion-rate formula is:

<div class="formula"><span>Conversion Rate</span><i>=</i><span class="frac"><span>Conversions</span><span>Visitors or Leads</span></span><i>× 100</i></div>

For example, if 1,000 people visit a landing page and 40 submit an enquiry:

<div class="formula"><span class="frac"><span>40</span><span>1,000</span></span><i>× 100 =</i><b>4%</b></div>

The landing page has a 4% enquiry conversion rate.

## 3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)

Customer Acquisition Cost measures how much a business spends to acquire a new customer.

A simplified formula is:

<div class="formula"><span>CAC</span><i>=</i><span class="frac"><span>Acquisition Costs</span><span>New Customers</span></span></div>

For example: if a business spends $2,000 on sales and marketing activities and acquires 20 new customers:

<div class="formula"><span>CAC</span><i>=</i><span class="frac"><span>$2,000</span><span>20</span></span><i>=</i><b>$100</b></div>

The average acquisition cost is $100 per customer.

For a more complete calculation, include relevant advertising, marketing, and sales expenses over the same period.

## 4. Return on Advertising Spend (ROAS)

ROAS measures revenue attributed to advertising relative to advertising expenditure.

Formula:

<div class="formula"><span>ROAS</span><i>=</i><span class="frac"><span>Attributed Ad Revenue</span><span>Ad Spend</span></span></div>

For example:

- Advertising expenditure: $1,000.
- Attributed revenue: $5,000.

ROAS:

<div class="formula"><span class="frac"><span>$5,000</span><span>$1,000</span></span><i>=</i><b>5</b></div>

That represents $5 in attributed revenue for every $1 spent on advertising.

Remember, ROAS does not account for every business expense. It is not the same as profit.

## 5. Customer Lifetime Value (CLV)

Some customers purchase once. Others continue buying for years.

Customer Lifetime Value estimates the revenue or value a business receives from a customer over the relationship.

This metric helps businesses understand how much they can reasonably invest in acquiring and retaining customers.

A simple model is:

<div class="formula"><span>CLV</span><i>=</i><span>Average Purchase Value</span><i>×</i><span>Purchase Frequency</span><i>×</i><span>Customer Lifespan</span></div>

Businesses should choose a model that reflects their actual revenue and retention patterns.

## 6. Email Marketing Performance

Email marketing can help businesses nurture leads and maintain customer relationships.

Track:

- Delivery rate.
- Click-through rate.
- Conversion rate.
- Unsubscribe rate.
- Revenue attributed to campaigns.

Open rates can be useful as a directional metric, but privacy features and automated email loading can make them unreliable.

Focus more on meaningful actions such as clicks, enquiries, purchases, and qualified conversions.

## 7. Website Performance

Your website should support your business goals.

Useful metrics include:

- Organic search traffic.
- Landing page conversion rate.
- Traffic sources.
- Enquiry submissions.
- Mobile performance.
- Website engagement.
- Technical errors.

Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console can help businesses understand website activity and search performance. As AI-powered search grows, it's also worth watching referral traffic from AI platforms. See [What AI Search Means for Small Business Visibility](insights/ai-search-small-business-visibility-2027/).

## How Often Should You Review Marketing Performance?

A useful reporting schedule could look like this:

| Frequency | What to review |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | Leads, campaign issues, enquiries |
| Weekly | Traffic, conversions, campaign performance |
| Monthly | CAC, ROAS, customer acquisition, revenue |
| Quarterly | Strategy, channel effectiveness, budget allocation |

The right frequency depends on your business and marketing activity.

## Final Thoughts

Marketing success should be measured by the value it creates for a business.

Engagement and follower growth can help assess brand awareness, but they need to connect to meaningful business objectives.
