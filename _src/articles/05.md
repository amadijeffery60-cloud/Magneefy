What happens when the person responsible for an important business task is unavailable?

Can someone else complete the task correctly?

Or does the entire process stop because only one person knows how everything works?

This is a common challenge for growing businesses.

Important processes often exist in someone's memory rather than in a documented system.

That creates unnecessary dependency, inconsistent work, and difficulties when new employees join.

Standard Operating Procedures, commonly called SOPs, help solve this problem.

## What Are Standard Operating Procedures?

An SOP is a documented set of instructions explaining how to complete a specific business task or process.

It provides employees with a clear reference for performing routine activities consistently.

Examples include:

- How to respond to new customer enquiries.
- How to onboard a new client.
- How to process an invoice.
- How to publish social media content.
- How to handle customer complaints.
- How to prepare weekly reports.

An effective SOP should explain what needs to happen, who is responsible, and what the expected result looks like.

## 1. SOPs Reduce Dependency on Individual Employees

When processes exist only in someone's memory, the business becomes dependent on that person.

If they leave, take time off, or change roles, important information can disappear.

Documented procedures help transfer knowledge across the team.

Employees can reference the instructions instead of repeatedly asking the same questions.

## 2. SOPs Improve Consistency

Customers expect a consistent experience.

If one employee follows one process while another follows a completely different approach, service quality can vary.

SOPs establish clear expectations.

For example, a customer support SOP might explain:

- How quickly enquiries should be acknowledged.
- What information should be collected.
- How complaints should be escalated.
- When a manager should become involved.

This makes customer interactions more consistent.

## 3. SOPs Make Employee Onboarding Easier

New employees need time to understand how a business operates.

Without documentation, training often depends entirely on another employee's availability.

SOPs provide a reference library for new team members.

They can learn:

- Which tools the business uses.
- How daily tasks are completed.
- Where documents are stored.
- Who handles specific responsibilities.
- How work should be submitted.

This makes onboarding more structured.

## 4. SOPs Make Automation Easier

This is one of the biggest advantages.

You cannot reliably automate a process that nobody understands.

Before automating a workflow, document how the process currently works.

Identify:

- What triggers the process.
- What information is required.
- Which actions happen next.
- Who approves important decisions.
- What happens when something goes wrong.

Once the process is clear, automation tools can handle suitable repetitive steps. If you're not sure where to start, see [7 Business Tasks You Should Automate](insights/business-tasks-to-automate/).

Documentation and automation work best together.

## 5. SOPs Improve Accountability

Every process should have clear ownership.

A useful SOP identifies:

- The responsible person.
- The task deadline.
- Required inputs.
- Expected outputs.
- Approval requirements.
- Escalation procedures.

This helps managers identify where delays happen and who needs support.

## How to Create Your First SOP

You do not need complicated software.

Start with these six steps.

### Step 1: Choose One Repetitive Task

Select a process your business performs regularly. For example, handling new customer enquiries, one of the most common places businesses [lose leads](insights/why-businesses-lose-leads/).

### Step 2: Document Every Step

Write down what happens from beginning to end.

### Step 3: Assign Responsibility

Identify who handles each stage.

### Step 4: Add Tools and Resources

List the software, templates, and documents needed.

### Step 5: Test the Instructions

Ask another team member to follow the SOP without additional explanations.

If they cannot complete the task, improve the documentation.

### Step 6: Review and Update

Business processes change. Review SOPs regularly and assign someone to maintain them.

## Where Should You Store Your SOPs?

Choose a platform your team can access and maintain.

Common options include:

- Notion.
- Google Drive.
- Microsoft SharePoint.
- Confluence.

Organize documentation by department or process.

Use clear titles, permissions, and version information so employees can find the correct instructions.

## Final Thoughts

SOPs are not only for large corporations.

Small businesses need documented processes too.
